The big picture

This route trades altitude for desert and desert for granite: you'll start in the Colorado high country, drop into Utah's red-rock canyon country around Moab, thread the Mighty 5 toward Zion, peer into the Grand Canyon, hit the neon of Las Vegas, climb the Eastern Sierra into Yosemite, and coast down to the bay.

The pacing front-loads the parks (where you'll want full days) and saves the longest drive — Las Vegas to Yosemite — for Day 9. Each day below lists the drive, the stops worth pulling over for, where to sleep, and a tip to make it smoother.

01
Colorado Springs → Moab, UT

Over the Rockies into Red-Rock Country

~5.5 hrs · 350 mi

Royal Gorge Bridge Optional

A quick detour near Cañon City to one of the highest suspension bridges in the U.S., spanning 955 ft above the Arkansas River. Walk across or skip the park and just admire the gorge.

Black Canyon of the Gunnison NP Highlight

Near Montrose — a dramatically narrow, sheer-walled chasm that's far less crowded than the famous parks. The South Rim Road's overlooks (Gunnison Point, Painted Wall) take an hour or two and are genuinely breathtaking.

Colorado National Monument Optional

Just outside Grand Junction, Rim Rock Drive winds past red sandstone canyons and monoliths — a perfect golden-hour warm-up for the desert ahead if you have daylight left.

Sleep

Moab — the basecamp for Arches & Canyonlands.

Tip

Leave early. It's a long mountain-to-desert day, so pick one or two stops rather than all three.

02
Moab, UT — basecamp

Arches National Park

Local · short drives

Delicate Arch Must-do

Utah's icon. A 3-mile round-trip hike with no shade leads to the freestanding arch framing the La Sal Mountains — magic at sunset. Go early morning or late afternoon to dodge the heat.

The Windows Section Highlight

North & South Window, Turret Arch, and Double Arch are all reachable on short, easy paths clustered together — the best arch-density-per-step in the park.

Devils Garden & Landscape Arch

An easy walk to Landscape Arch, one of the longest natural stone spans in the world. Continue further for Partition and Double O arches if you've got energy.

Balanced Rock & Park Avenue

Two quick roadside stops — a boulder perched impossibly on a spire, and a short walk between towering sandstone walls near the entrance.

Sleep

Moab again — dinner and drinks on Main Street.

Tip

A timed-entry reservation is required to enter Arches in peak season (spring–fall mornings). Book ahead at recreation.gov.

03
Moab, UT — basecamp

Canyonlands & Dead Horse Point

Local · ~45 min each way

Mesa Arch at Sunrise Must-do

In the Island in the Sky district. The rising sun lights the underside of this cliff-edge arch into a glowing red — the most photographed sunrise in Utah. Arrive 45 min early to claim a spot.

Grand View Point & Green River Overlook Highlight

Sweeping overlooks across a 1,000-ft-deep maze of canyons carved by the Colorado and Green rivers. The short Grand View rim walk is the payoff.

Dead Horse Point State Park Sunset

A gooseneck bend of the Colorado River 2,000 ft below — arguably an even better view than the national park, and the perfect sunset finale before heading back to Moab.

Optional adrenaline

Swap an afternoon for a Colorado River raft trip, a 4x4 tour on Hell's Revenge, or mountain biking — Moab is the adventure capital of the desert.

Sleep

Moab — last night before the long push west.

Tip

Fill the tank and stock water/snacks tonight — tomorrow crosses remote stretches of central Utah.

04
Moab → Zion (Springdale), UT

Across Utah to Zion's Gates

~5 hrs direct · ~6.5 hrs scenic

Capitol Reef NP Scenic route

The least-visited of Utah's Mighty 5. Drive the Scenic Drive past towering cliffs, see ancient petroglyphs, and grab a fresh fruit pie at the historic Gifford Homestead in Fruita.

Scenic Byway 12 Optional

Frequently rated one of America's most beautiful drives — winding over slickrock ridges and through Grand Staircase-Escalante. Adds time but unforgettable.

Direct route via I-70 / US-89

If you'd rather arrive early and rest up for Zion, the interstate route is fast and still scenic through the San Rafael Swell.

Sleep

Springdale — the walkable town right at Zion's doorstep.

Tip

Decide the route by your energy: the Capitol Reef detour is glorious but you'll be tired for Zion's big hikes tomorrow.

05
Zion National Park, UT

Into the Great Canyon

Shuttle-based · no driving in canyon

Angels Landing Must-do*

The legendary chain-assisted climb along a knife-edge ridge to a 1,488-ft perch over the canyon. Requires a permit via seasonal lottery ($6 to apply) — and a head for heights. No permit? Scout Lookout still delivers huge views.

The Narrows Must-do

Wade straight up the Virgin River between 1,000-ft walls that close in around you. Rent neoprene socks, canyon shoes, and a walking stick from a Springdale outfitter. Start early.

Canyon Overlook Trail

A short (1-mile) trail near the Mt. Carmel tunnel with a massive payoff view down Zion Canyon — great for a lower-effort evening if your legs are spent.

Sleep

Springdale once more.

Tip

Apply for the Angels Landing permit weeks ahead (a day-before lottery also runs). The canyon shuttle is mandatory most of the year — park in town and ride in.

06
Zion → Page → Grand Canyon, AZ

Slot Canyons & the South Rim

~4.5 hrs driving + stops

Horseshoe Bend Highlight

In Page, AZ — a short walk from the lot to the rim above the Colorado River's perfect 270° bend, 1,000 ft below. Best light is midday-to-afternoon when sun reaches the canyon floor.

Antelope Canyon Book ahead

The surreal, wave-like slot canyon (Upper or Lower) glowing with shafts of light. Only accessible on a Navajo-guided tour — reserve weeks in advance, especially in summer.

Grand Canyon South Rim — Sunset Must-do

Roll into the park by evening and catch first light fade at Mather Point or Hopi Point. The first glimpse over the rim is a moment you don't forget.

Sleep

Grand Canyon Village (book early) or Tusayan just outside.

Tip

Arizona doesn't observe daylight saving — your phone clock may jump. Plan tour and sunset times accordingly.

07
Grand Canyon National Park, AZ

A Full Day on the Rim

Shuttle & short drives

Mather Point at Sunrise Must-do

The classic overlook, steps from the visitor center, with the canyon catching the first golden light. Then stroll the paved Rim Trail toward Yavapai Point for quieter angles.

Bright Angel Trail Highlight

Descend a bit into the canyon for a different perspective — the 1.5-Mile Resthouse is a great turnaround. Remember: going down is optional, coming up is mandatory. Carry plenty of water.

Desert View Drive & Watchtower

A 25-mile scenic drive east past panoramic pullouts, ending at the Desert View Watchtower designed by Mary Colter — climb it for a 360° view.

Hermit Road for Sunset Sunset

Take the free shuttle out to Hopi or Mohave Point for the day's grand finale as the canyon walls glow orange and purple.

Sleep

Grand Canyon / Tusayan, or push to Williams on Route 66.

Tip

The free park shuttles are the easiest way around the rim — parking near viewpoints fills fast by mid-morning.

08
Grand Canyon → Las Vegas, NV

Route 66, the Dam & the Strip

~4.5 hrs · 270 mi

Route 66 in Seligman Optional

The kitschy birthplace of the historic Route 66 revival — vintage diners, neon signs, and old gas pumps. A fun, nostalgic leg-stretch.

Hoover Dam Highlight

An engineering marvel straddling the AZ/NV line. Walk across the Mike O'Callaghan–Pat Tillman Bridge for the best view of the dam and Black Canyon below.

The Strip after dark Must-do

Watch the Bellagio fountains, wander the Venetian's canals, and soak in the spectacle. After a week of desert silence, the neon hits different. A proper meal and a real bed await.

Sleep

A Strip hotel — splurge a little; you've earned it.

Tip

Crossing into Nevada you gain an hour (back to Pacific time). Hoover Dam lots fill early — arrive before midday.

09
Las Vegas → Eastern Sierra / Yosemite, CA

The Long Haul to the Sierra

~7.5 hrs (longest day)

Mono Lake Highlight

Near Lee Vining — an ancient saline lake studded with otherworldly tufa towers (limestone spires). The South Tufa boardwalk is a surreal, quick stop before the climb into the park.

Tioga Pass & Tuolumne Meadows Summer only

The spectacular eastern entrance climbs to 9,943 ft past alpine lakes and granite domes. Tioga Pass (CA-120) is only open roughly late May/June–October — check status before you go.

Winter alternative If pass is closed

If Tioga is closed, you'll route the long way around via Bakersfield and enter through the south (Hwy 41) — budget closer to 9+ hours and consider an overnight in Mammoth or Bishop.

Sleep

Inside the park, Lee Vining, Mariposa, or Oakhurst — book very early.

Tip

This is the toughest driving day — fuel up in Vegas and again at Tonopah/Lee Vining; long stretches have no services.

10
Yosemite → San Francisco, CA

Granite Giants to the Golden Gate

~4 hrs · 190 mi (Valley → SF)

Tunnel View Must-do

The postcard: El Capitan, Bridalveil Fall, and Half Dome framed in one sweeping vista. Best in morning light. The single most iconic view in Yosemite.

Yosemite Valley Loop Highlight

Drive the valley floor beneath El Capitan and Half Dome, walk to the base of Bridalveil Fall, and stop at Cook's Meadow for the classic Half Dome reflection.

San Francisco — the finish line Must-do

Arrive by evening for the Golden Gate Bridge, Fisherman's Wharf and the Pier 39 sea lions, a cable-car ride, and a well-earned celebratory dinner. Journey complete.

Sleep

San Francisco — you made it coast to coast (well, Rockies to coast).

Tip

Leave Yosemite Valley by early afternoon to beat traffic into the Bay Area. A peak-season day-use reservation may be required to enter the park.

Know Before You Go

A few things that make this trip run smoothly.

🎟 Permits & Reservations

Book these well ahead: Arches timed-entry ticket (peak season), Angels Landing permit lottery, Antelope Canyon Navajo-guided tour, and a Yosemite peak-season day-use reservation. Park lodges sell out months in advance.

🏔 Get the Parks Pass

You'll hit 5+ national park sites. The America the Beautiful annual pass ($80) pays for itself almost immediately and covers everyone in your vehicle.

❄ Seasonal Watch-out

Tioga Pass (Day 9) typically opens only late May/June through October. Outside that window you must detour around the southern Sierra — adding hours. Always check current road status before driving.

🕐 Time Zones

You'll cross from Mountain to Pacific time. Note Arizona doesn't observe daylight saving, so clocks can shift unexpectedly around the Grand Canyon and Page.

💧 Desert Smarts

Carry far more water than you think — summer rim and canyon temps are extreme. Fuel up whenever you're below half a tank; some stretches (central Utah, Nevada) have long gaps between stations.

🌅 Beat the Crowds

Sunrise is your secret weapon — Mesa Arch, Delicate Arch, and Mather Point are transcendent at dawn and mobbed by mid-morning. Early starts also dodge afternoon desert heat.
